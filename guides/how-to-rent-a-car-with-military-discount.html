How to rent a car with a military discount — leave vs. orders, and renting under 25

Last verification pass: November 27, 2026 (Pacific Time).

Verified Nov 27, 2026

Holiday leave is when a lot of young service members rent a car for the first time — and when the counter surprises happen: the “military discount” that still comes with a daily under-25 fee, or the government rate that only applies on orders. This guide sorts it out from the rental companies’ and the Defense Travel Management Office’s own pages. Every rule below was read on the official page on November 27, 2026.

Step 1: Decide which kind of trip this is

Enterprise puts it plainly: Government Leisure Travel is off-duty travel or personal needs; Official Government Travel is for official business or temporary duty. They are booked differently and come with different protection.

The government program is for official business only, per DTMO. Using it for leave is not what it is for — and the included insurance is tied to official use. For leave, book the brand’s leisure military rate.

Step 2: If you are under 25, read the age rules before you book

This is the question we see most from junior enlisted members heading home on leave. The short version from the official pages: a military leisure rate does not by itself remove the young-renter fee. Orders change the picture.

Avis’s page names the government personnel the order rule covers as “FEMA, U.S. Postal Dept., Navy, and Army.” If you serve in another branch, ask Avis before you rely on it. On official travel under the government program, DTMO says drivers 18–20 may be charged an underage fee, must be listed on the rental agreement, and that fee is reimbursable; there is no extra charge for additional authorized drivers 21 and older.

Step 3: Pick the leisure military rate that fits

  • Enterprise — 5% off base rates for Government Leisure Travel. No military-association membership required. Eligible: current federal employees, active-duty members, retired employees, and veterans. Accepted at Enterprise locations worldwide, per Enterprise’s FAQ.
  • Hertz — book through Hertz’s Government and Military page and the leisure government discount code (CDP) is filled in for you. Hertz does not publish the percentage. Listed leisure benefits: discounted rates, space-available upgrades, and complimentary Hertz Gold Plus Rewards membership. Hertz names active-duty military, National Guard, reservists, and eligible veterans.
  • Avis — Avis says active military, veterans, and their families can receive military discounts when they enroll in WeSalute. Its official offers page also lists AWD T765700: up to 25% off base rates for veteran and military family. Enter the AWD at the start of the booking, not the end. Avis allows one AWD plus one coupon code per reservation, not two AWDs.

Compare the final total, not the headline percentage. Taxes, airport fees, and any under-25 fee are added on top, and a 5% discount on the base rate can lose to a weekend sale rate.

Step 4: At the counter

  • A driver’s license held for at least a year (Enterprise and Hertz both say so) and a credit or debit card in your name. Enterprise says debit cards require ID and address verification; some Hertz neighborhood locations take only credit cards.
  • Military or government ID — Enterprise says it may be requested at pickup for the leisure rate.
  • On orders: your travel authorization, and check that your Department or Agency name is printed on the contract. That is how DTMO says you know you are covered by the program.

Common questions

Is there a Hertz military code I should copy from a forum?

You do not need one. Hertz pre-populates the government CDP when you start from its official Government and Military page. We do not publish unofficial codes.

Can my spouse rent with the military rate?

It depends on the company. Avis’s AWD T765700 is described as for “veteran and military family,” and Avis’s WeSalute line names families. Enterprise’s eligibility list names federal employees, active-duty members, retired employees, and veterans — not spouses. Hertz’s page names service members and eligible veterans. Ask at booking if the renter is not the service member.

Does USAA membership help?

Enterprise says USAA members have a separate member discount and benefits, and Hertz’s young-renter page says eligible USAA members aged 20–24 renting under the applicable CDP may have the Young Renter fee waived. Check USAA’s own member page for its current rental terms before you book.

Can I put a leave trip on the government rate?

No. DTMO describes the U.S. Government Rental Car Program as open to federal employees and service members traveling on official business. For leave, use a leisure military rate.
