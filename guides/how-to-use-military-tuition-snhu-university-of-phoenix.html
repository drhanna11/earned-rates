How to use SNHU or University of Phoenix military tuition (talk to your ESO first)

Last verification pass: November 23, 2026 (Pacific Time).

Verified Nov 23, 2026

Southern New Hampshire University and University of Phoenix both publish a military undergraduate tuition rate of $250 per credit hour — the same figure as the Army’s Tuition Assistance ceiling per semester hour. That match is why so many service members start here. But the order of steps matters: if you are serving, your Education Services Officer (ESO) or installation education center comes before any enrollment call, and graduate programs cost more than TA covers. This guide follows each school’s official page and official military education sources as we read them on November 23, 2026.

Step 1: Talk to your ESO or education center first

University of Phoenix’s own military page says it plainly: active and Reserve military members and their families are directed to speak with their assigned educational services officer or approved installation staff before enrolling. National Guard and Reserve members without an ESO on their installation can contact their regional office. That is good advice for any school, including SNHU.

  • Why it matters. TA is approved course by course through your service’s system, on your service’s rules and deadlines. The Army, for example, now requires first-time users to complete ArmyIgnitED training and use a decision support tool before requesting TA, and Soldiers need supervisor or commander-representative approval (per the official Army Benefits TA page).
  • Timing. The Nebraska Army National Guard’s official TA page says courses can be requested up to 60 days before the start date and must be requested at least 7 days before it. Your branch may differ — your ESO will know.
  • Check the school. Use the Department’s TA DECIDE tool on Military OneSource to confirm a school is eligible for TA and has signed the Voluntary Education Partnership Memorandum of Understanding, and to compare cost and quality data.

New this week: a GAO report released November 20, 2026 (GAO-27-107854) found that the Navy and Air Force do not require bases to brief tuition assistance at orientation for newly assigned members, and that some service members were unaware of the benefit. If you just PCSed and nobody mentioned TA, that is why. Ask your education office directly.

Step 2: Know what TA covers — and the graduate gap

Tuition Assistance is set by each service under DoD policy. The figures we can quote from official Army sources tonight: up to $250 per semester hour, up to 18 semester hours, and $4,500 per fiscal year (Nebraska Army National Guard’s Federal TA page; the Army Benefits site confirms the $4,500 annual cap and 18 hours under ALARACT 102/2025). Eighteen semester hours is six 3-credit courses a year. Navy, Air Force, Space Force, Marine Corps, and Coast Guard members should confirm their own caps and eligibility rules with their ESO; GAO notes some services require a certain time in service before you can use TA.

Step 4: Confirm the rate in writing before your first course

  1. SNHU: confirm your status qualifies for the military rate. SNHU’s military page names Active Duty, National Guard, Selected Reserve, and active-duty spouses; its 2026–2027 online tuition table labels the same rate “Active Duty Military.” If you are Guard, Reserve, or a spouse, email MilTA@snhu.edu and get the answer in writing.
  2. University of Phoenix: the military page shows two tabs. The Service Members tab carries the $250 per credit undergraduate rate. The Veterans tab shows higher per-course tuition ($426 per credit hour for associate and bachelor’s). Make sure your enrollment counselor applies the right one, and ask for the resource-fee waiver if you are an undergraduate using TA.
  3. Send your TA authorization to the school the way your service and the school require, before the course starts.

If you are a veteran or a spouse

  • Veterans: TA is for serving members. Veterans generally use GI Bill benefits; confirm yours at VA.gov, not from a recruiter’s summary. University of Phoenix’s page directs veterans to the VA for benefit details. SNHU lists the Post-9/11 GI Bill, Yellow Ribbon, Montgomery GI Bill, and VR&E among benefits it works with.
  • Military spouses: SNHU publishes a spouse rate for spouses of active-duty members (same $250 / $470 per credit). SNHU’s page also points spouses to the SpouseWorks Scholarship (formerly MyCAA), which it describes as up to $4,000 of tuition assistance for eligible military spouses — confirm eligibility on the program’s official page. University of Phoenix does not publish a spouse tuition rate on its military page.

If orders pull you out mid-term

TA is earned in proportion to the time you attend. SNHU’s published policy: it returns unearned TA based on days attended, and once you have attended more than 60% of the term you are considered to have earned 100% of the TA. Any balance created by a TA return is the student’s responsibility — except that if you stop attending because of a military service obligation during the term, SNHU says it will remove you from the coursework and cover the amount owed so no debt is incurred, with documentation such as current orders or a signed command letter. A previously existing obligation does not qualify. Ask University of Phoenix for its equivalent policy in writing before you enroll.

Common questions

Will TA pay my whole bill at SNHU or University of Phoenix?

For undergraduate courses, each school’s published military tuition is $250 per credit hour — at, not above, the Army’s $250 ceiling — so tuition may be covered within your annual cap. Books and other fees are separate (University of Phoenix waives its undergraduate resource fee for eligible TA users). Graduate courses at both schools cost more per credit than the ceiling, so expect to pay the difference or use another benefit.

Can I combine the military rate with another school discount?

Not at SNHU: students receiving the military rate are not eligible for additional SNHU tuition discounts. University of Phoenix says its listed costs do not include transfer credits or scholarships; ask which ones apply to you.

Do these rates change?

Yes. SNHU says its rates are effective as of June 2026 and reviewed annually; University of Phoenix last updated its military page on November 2, 2026. We re-check both pages on a regular cadence and date every check.
