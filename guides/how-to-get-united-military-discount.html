How to get the United military discount (5% off) — and what spouses flying alone can still claim

Last verification pass: November 25, 2026 (Pacific Time).

Verified Nov 25, 2026

United is the one major U.S. airline that publishes a fixed leisure-fare discount for active-duty members: 5% off the base fare on United-operated flights. It is a real, earned benefit — and it has rules that are easy to trip over at holiday booking time. This guide walks through them from United’s own pages, then answers the question we saw from a military family this Thanksgiving week: what can a spouse flying home alone, while the service member is deployed, actually claim? Every term below was read on the airline’s official page on November 25, 2026.

Who qualifies for United’s 5%

The leisure discount is for active-duty members of the U.S. Army, Navy, Air Force, Marines, Space Force, and Coast Guard. United’s broader military benefits (bags, boarding, club access) also list academy cadets and midshipmen, USPHS and NOAA Commissioned Officers, Reserve and National Guard members on active orders, and members discharged within 7 days of scheduled travel. Veterans and retirees are not named for the 5% fare; United’s page points veterans to a separate United Vacations package discount instead.

Step 1: Verify active-duty status in MileagePlus

Sign in to (or create) your MileagePlus account and verify your active-duty status in your profile, as United’s page directs. Do this before the holiday rush — not at the gate.

Step 2: Search on united.com or the app — and skip Basic Economy

After verification, eligible flights are marked with a discount applied. Choose a fare that is not Basic Economy, and check that every segment is operated by United or United Express.

Step 3: Put the service member on the reservation

The active-duty MileagePlus account holder must be on the itinerary. You can book up to nine passengers together — a deployed member’s family flying on its own cannot use the member’s account for the 5%.

Step 4: Count the bags, not just the fare

On a domestic trip the bag benefit can be worth more than the 5%. United’s military checked-bag table, which applies to U.S. military personnel and their dependents on United and United Express flights:

  • Personal travel: 3 bags with no service charge, up to 62 inches and 70 lbs each, all classes of service.
  • Military orders: 5 bags, up to 115 inches and 100 lbs each.
  • Military dependent with travel orders: 2 bags, 62 inches, 70 lbs each.
  • Dependents share these benefits when traveling on the same reservation. On orders, dependents can travel alone; without orders, a dependent traveling alone pays the normal allowance and fees.
  • If any part of the trip is on another airline — including Star Alliance partners — the military bag benefits do not apply.

United’s page also lists the pre-boarding group for active military, and United Club access “if there’s space available and you meet the access requirements.” The lounge-access page we read tonight does not spell out a military-specific rule, so ask at the club desk rather than counting on it. Full bag rules: United checked bags.

United Vacations (flight + hotel)

United’s military page says United Vacations offers a separate discount to active-duty and veteran U.S. military personnel on flight-and-hotel packages. It does not publish a percentage there, and it cannot be combined with the 5% leisure discount. The United Vacations military page returned an error to our checker tonight, so confirm the terms on that page when you book.

Common questions

Can veterans or retirees get United’s 5%?

Not under the published rules — the leisure discount names active-duty members of the six services. Veterans can look at the United Vacations military package discount.

Does the 5% work on partner or codeshare flights?

No. Flights must be fully operated by United or United Express, and the bag benefits drop off if any segment is on another airline.

Is there a promo code?

No code is published. The discount appears after MileagePlus verification. Be wary of any site selling a “United military code.”
