How to claim a military auto rebate (GM, Ford, Toyota, Hyundai, Jeep)

Last verification pass: November 21, 2026 (Pacific Time).

Verified on November 21, 2026

A military rebate on a new vehicle is one of the largest single benefits in this directory — and one of the easiest to lose at the finance desk. Every program below is funded by the automaker and claimed through an online verification step before you sign: an authorization number for GM and Jeep, a certificate for Ford, ID.me for Toyota and Hyundai. This guide follows each automaker’s official page as we read it on November 21, 2026. Where a brand does not publish a dollar amount, we do not supply one.

Compare every official auto rebate

The order that protects your rebate

  1. Verify online first, before you visit. Use the automaker’s own page (linked below). Verification is usually quick, but document uploads can take longer, and GM requires the number before delivery.
  2. Read the vehicle and financing rules. Toyota requires Toyota Financial Services. Jeep lists specific models. GM and Hyundai exclude certain other affinity programs.
  3. Tell the dealer at first contact that you will use the manufacturer’s military program. GM notes dealer participation is voluntary; if one dealer will not participate, you may go to another.
  4. Settle the vehicle price, then apply the rebate. Ask for the military rebate as its own line on the buyer’s order so you can see it was applied.
  5. Bring what the program names — the authorization number, certificate email, or ID.me confirmation, plus any ID the program lists.
  6. Check the final paperwork before you sign. If the rebate is missing, stop and contact the program line listed on the official page.

Steps 3–6 are our practical advice for keeping the benefit visible. The program terms themselves belong to each automaker and are summarized brand by brand below.

GM: Chevrolet, Buick, GMC, and Cadillac

  1. Create a GM My Account, then return to gmmilitaryappreciation.com to finish registration.
  2. Verify through ID.me (USAA login, military service records, a .mil email, or document upload).
  3. Click Get Authorization Number. GM emails the number once approved. It is valid for 90 days; if it expires unused, the authorization returns to your account.
  4. Give the number to a participating U.S. GM dealer before you take delivery, with a copy of your driver’s license (license number redacted) showing the ZIP code and date of birth you used.

Who qualifies: Active Duty members, Reservists, National Guard members, all Veterans and Retirees of the U.S. Army, Navy, Air Force, Marine Corps, and Coast Guard, and their sponsored spouse. Limits: two eligible new vehicles per calendar year; keep each at least six months. Not stackable with the GM First Responder, Educator, Family First, Supplier, Dealership Employee, Supplier Discount for Friends, or Employee Purchase Program for Executive Referrals offers. Stationed overseas? GM says you can arrange it on the site, but you must purchase and take delivery in the United States.

Ford and Lincoln

  1. On fordrecognizesu.com, complete Step 1 – Find My Offer with your ZIP code. This is where your market’s cash amount appears.
  2. Complete Step 2 – Confirm My ID through ID.me. Ford says it typically takes under a minute, or up to about four minutes with a document upload.
  3. Bring the email confirmation showing your Certificate to the Ford dealer or Lincoln retailer. If the email does not arrive, check spam; the dealer can also search the certificate, and Program Headquarters is at (888) 322-5862.

Who qualifies (U.S. residents): active duty, including National Guard, reservists serving on active duty, and DEP members; retirees (regular, reserve, TERA, medical); and honorably discharged veterans within 24 months of separation. Spouses, surviving spouses, and U.S.-resident household members may register. Ford adds that veterans may qualify for a special regional incentive regardless of separation date — check by ZIP. State Guard is not eligible. Limit: five eligible new vehicles per calendar year. The FAQ is dated January 6, 2026–January 4, 2027. Overseas: Ford points to Military AutoSource.

Toyota

Toyota offers a $500 rebate toward any new Toyota purchased or leased through your dealer and Toyota Financial Services. You must be verified. Eligible: U.S. military personnel (active-duty and inactive reserve), household members of eligible personnel including Gold Star families, U.S. military retirees, and U.S. military veterans within two years of discharge. Toyota’s page says ID.me verification is not available in Alabama, Florida, Georgia, North Carolina, and South Carolina — ask the dealer how verification works in those states. Toyota’s official rebate page.

Hyundai

Hyundai offers a $500 bonus toward an eligible new Hyundai (all new models are listed as eligible), January 7 through December 31, 2026. The customer or spouse must be active duty, Reserve/National Guard, veteran, or retired at the start of the program period, and must verify with ID.me before purchase. The bonus is applied toward MSRP and is not available as cash; on a lease it goes to the amount due at signing or capitalized cost reduction, and on a loan to the down payment. Not for fleet, service rental, or demo vehicles; vehicle must come out of dealer stock; not combinable with Hyundai Circle plan sales or the First Responders Program. Hyundai’s official military page.

Jeep

Jeep offers a $500 Military Bonus Cash Allowance on select 2026 and 2025 models to Active, Active Reserve, Retired Military, Retired Military Reserve, 100% Disabled Veterans, and honorably discharged veterans within 12 months of discharge. Use Get Military Authorization Number (or the disabled-military button) on Jeep’s page and present the code to a franchised FCA US LLC dealer in the United States at purchase or lease. Surviving spouses of deceased personnel who meet the criteria may also qualify. Personnel stationed overseas are pointed to Military AutoSource. Jeep’s official incentive page.

Nissan (flagged: partial terms)

Nissan’s official page states that qualifying military personnel can get $500 cash back toward the lease or purchase of a qualifying new Nissan, that it can be combined with all current national and regional incentives, and that proof of eligibility is required. The detailed eligibility footnote on that page loads by script and did not render for us tonight, so we are not listing who qualifies. Confirm on Nissan’s official military page and with the dealer.

Common questions

Can I combine two automaker military programs on one vehicle?

Each program applies to its own brand. Within a brand, read the stacking rules: GM lists specific programs it cannot stack with, Hyundai excludes the First Responders Program and Hyundai Circle plan sales, and Ford tells you to ask the dealer about compatibility.

Can my spouse use it?

It depends on the brand. GM allows a sponsored spouse; Ford lets spouses, surviving spouses, and household members register; Toyota includes household members and Gold Star families; Hyundai says the customer or spouse; Jeep names surviving spouses of deceased personnel who meet the criteria.

I’m separating soon. Should I wait?

Check the window first. Jeep’s veteran window is 12 months from discharge; Ford’s is 24 months from separation (with a possible regional veteran offer); Toyota’s is two years. GM lists all veterans.

Is there an affiliate link here?

No. These are manufacturer programs with no public affiliate program on the vehicle sale. Every button on this page goes to the automaker’s own site.
